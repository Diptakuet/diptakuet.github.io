Most dynamic detectors for microarchitectural attacks rely on hardware performance counters, which add significant overhead and can monitor only a few events at once. MAD-EN instead uses system-wide energy consumption traces collected through Intel's Running Average Power Limit (RAPL) interface. A CNN-based classifier detects 10 attack types spanning 15 variants, including Spectre and Meltdown, with a best F1 score of 0.999. Once an anomaly is flagged, individual attacks are distinguished with 98% accuracy. Compared with performance counter-based detection, MAD-EN reduces performance overhead by 69.3%.
